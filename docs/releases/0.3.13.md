# Pippa 0.3.13

<!-- Release preparation: publish only with matching CI, signed/notarized packaging and a verified update. Test limits are documented below and in the candidate assessment. -->

Pippa is a private AI agent that lives on your Mac. Ask her something, or drop a letter, a PDF, a folder or a few photos on her, and she does the work: she explains what a letter wants from you and by when, finds files, drafts replies in Mail and tidies up folders. Her built-in AI runs on your Mac: no account, no subscription.

**You need** a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 15 or later. **Download** `Pippa.dmg` below, open it and drag Pippa to Applications. Photo and PDF actions work before AI setup. For AI chat, Pippa asks before downloading about 3 to 6 GB, depending on your Mac's memory.

**New in Pippa 0.3.13**

- **Check an uncertain reminder.** Choose **Check status** to inspect an earlier reminder without creating another one, or **Open Reminders** to look yourself. Pippa keeps the result with the original conversation. If she cannot confirm it, she says so.
- **Repair access from the conversation.** A supported Mac action or protected-folder read blocked by privacy settings offers **Open System Settings** for the relevant access. Your conversation stays open. Visiting Settings does not automatically repeat an action.
- **Photos access when it is needed.** Pippa can ask for access to photo previews from the conversation, even when it was skipped during setup. Choosing **Later** keeps the search results available without previews.
- **More careful Mail drafts.** Pippa checks a reply's fields after saving and keeps an unconfirmed result visible when Mail does not answer. An incomplete search no longer counts as a unique original message. New messages remain clearly described as open compose windows.

**Test limits:** physical 8 GB and 16 GB Macs and a fresh macOS permission profile were unavailable. Real Mail compose/reply acceptance remains unverified. The successful native reminder restart/recovery test used a separately instrumented signed candidate; it is not a claim that every shipped-app interaction was tested. Model defaults are unchanged.
